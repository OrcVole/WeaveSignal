<Feature id="room" kicker="The room and the desk" title="Everyone hears the show. Nobody hears themselves.">
  <p>Presenters, guests and callers join one room from a browser, over WebRTC, with TURN for the networks that block it. The desk joins the same room, mixes the programme, and sends each person a mix-minus: everyone they should hear, never their own voice. On loopback a guest's return arrives 69 to 77 ms after the voice.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Cue, talkback, mute and a held talk key, from the keyboard; the keys are printed on the desk.</li>
    <li>A profanity delay with a Dump key, and a limiter on every mix.</li>
    <li>What each role hears is fixed by the desk: <a href="/reference/who-hears-whom/">Who hears whom</a>.</li>
    <li>A person's failed link is rebuilt on its own; only the programme is fatal.</li>
  </ul>
  <Shot slot="media" src={stage} alt="The desk's stage with four tiles ON AIR: Eilidh Fraser-MacLeod the presenter, Gregor Stewart and Kirsty Wilson as guests, and Niall Anderson, a caller sent by the screener, cued in the presenter's headphones with the screener's line under his name. Finlay Grant and playout stand by below." caption="A caller on the stage beside the presenter and two guests; Niall is cued so Eilidh hears him first." />
</Feature>

<Feature id="callers" kicker="Callers, screened" title="Callers ring from the player page. The screener answers first." layout="phones" side="left">
  <p>A listener calls from the station's page with a phone or computer microphone; there is no phone number. The call-in form takes a name, one line for the producer and consent to record, and a proof of work stands in for a CAPTCHA.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Each caller waits in a booth of their own: they hear the programme, see nobody, and nobody sees them.</li>
    <li>The screener talks to them off air, writes the line the presenter sees, and sends them to the desk or back to the queue.</li>
    <li>Someone banned before is marked before they are heard.</li>
    <li>Callers sound like a phone line by default; a station can turn that off.</li>
  </ul>
  <Shot slot="media" src={player} size="phone" alt="The listener's page on a phone: Kettle FM with its ON AIR lamp, The Morning Kettle with Rory Bruce live now, a Listen live button, and a Talk to the studio panel with a Call the studio button." caption="The player page, with the call-in panel." />
  <Shot slot="media" src={screener} size="phone" alt="The screener's queue: four callers waiting, Dougal Thomson, Jessie Brown, Niall Anderson and Tavish, who is marked BANNED BEFORE; below, Dougal's card with one line for Eilidh, the consent record, a note for the presenter, the name on air and the Send to desk, To queue and Drop keys." caption="The screener's queue and a caller's card." />
</Feature>

<Feature id="on-air" kicker="On air" title="Playout holds the air. The lamp tells the truth.">
  <p>Between shows, playout runs the schedule: a week of shows and automation blocks, each hour filled from a clock with rotation and separation rules. When the desk is quiet or away, listeners hear the fallback folder, or the default loop, and they stay connected through a playout or desk restart.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>One fixed Liquidsoap 2.5 script, driven over a socket, never generated.</li>
    <li>Ogg Opus for listeners, an MP3 stream for older players, a dead-air fallback with notices.</li>
    <li>A per-play log, listener hours, a legal logger that follows retention, and now playing for the station's site.</li>
    <li>A gain control towards −16 LUFS and a peak limiter before the stream.</li>
  </ul>
  <div class="ws-lamps" slot="below">
    <Lamp dark={onAirDark} light={onAirLight} alt="The ON AIR lamp, a red fill.">Listeners can hear you now, and only then.</Lamp>
    <Lamp dark={openDark} light={openLight} alt="The OPEN lamp, white, open on its right side.">Your microphone is live to the room while playout is down.</Lamp>
    <Lamp dark={autoDark} light={autoLight} alt="The AUTO lamp, hollow green.">Automation holds the air.</Lamp>
    <Lamp dark={playoutDownDark} light={playoutDownLight} alt="The PLAYOUT DOWN lamp, hollow with a warning glyph.">Nobody is listening to the room.</Lamp>
  </div>
  <Shot slot="media" src={week} alt="The studio app's Schedule tab showing the week of 2 to 8 November 2026: automation blocks for the daytime and evening playlists, live shows such as Kettle Breakfast, The Long Lunch and Kettle Drive, one marked CONFLICT, the current time line on Tuesday, and a side panel saying Nothing selected." caption="The week: live shows, automation blocks and one conflict, drawn by the schedule." />
</Feature>

<div class="ws-pair not-content">
  <Feature id="video" kicker="Video" title="Pictures follow the sound." layout="none" >
    <p>Video is off until a station turns it on. Cameras arrive on the same room connection; a vision mixer cuts to the loudest person on air and to the slate when nobody is. Frames are held behind the profanity delay with the sound, encoded to AV1 by the GPU or the CPU, and served as CMAF HLS with an audio-only toggle for viewers on a thin connection. So far it has been tested against stand-ins, not a studio.</p>
  </Feature>
  <Feature id="chat" kicker="The chat" title="A room for listeners, with the tools to keep it." layout="none" >
    <p>Listeners chat on the player page. Reading needs no account; posting needs a light one, signed in by an emailed code. A word filter, a rate limit and trusted-only links act first; reports, held messages and requests for review wait for the screener, who tells a chatter why. The minimum age and what is kept follow the station's jurisdiction. The station decides how it uses these tools; nothing here makes it compliant by itself.</p>
  </Feature>
</div>

<Feature id="shows" kicker="Shows, cards and podcasts" title="A show card for every episode, and a feed for every show." layout="phones" side="left">
  <p>A series fills in its own episodes from its weekly rule. Each one gets a card in the Poster or Slate layout at the sizes the web wants, drawn by the station itself, with alt text, so a link to the show looks right wherever it is shared.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>A podcast feed per show, with an Opus alternate beside the MP3.</li>
    <li>Corrections to an aired episode, logged.</li>
    <li>Retention by jurisdiction: presets for the UK, Ireland, the EU, the US, Canada and Australia, holds for what must be kept, and a sweeper for the rest.</li>
  </ul>
  <Shot slot="media" src={poster} size="phone" alt="The card editor for Saturday Night Kettle, series 3, episode 3: the Poster layout at 1,200 by 630 for a link preview, with the show's date, title, episode name, presenters Shona Campbell and Catriona MacKenzie-Buchanan, the guests, and the card's alt text under it." caption="The Poster card for an episode, with its alt text." />
  <Shot slot="media" src={slate} size="phone" alt="The same episode's card in the Slate layout: a light card with the station's name, the series and episode, Saturday Night Kettle, Ghost Train night, the date and time, and round initials for the hosts and guests; the layout is marked as being tried, with a Keep for the show button." caption="The Slate layout, tried for the same episode." />
</Feature>

<Feature id="self-hosting" kicker="Self-hosting" title="One install holds the whole station." layout="none">
  <p>The control plane, the desk, playout, the listener server, the chat and the room run as one install, with PostgreSQL. Only the web proxy and the room's media and TURN ports face the network; every port is a setting, and the installers find free ones. There is no published server image yet: for release one you build it from the repository.</p>
  <div class="ws-tiles" data-cols="4">
    <div class="ws-tile">
      <h3><a href="/how-to/install-on-cloudron/">Cloudron</a></h3>
      <p>One Cloudron app with the station behind Cloudron's login and its PostgreSQL addon. A staging package today; the app store comes after packaging.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="ws-tile">
      <h3><a href="/how-to/install-with-weavesignal-sh/">Compose</a></h3>
      <p><code>weavesignal.sh</code> finds Podman or Docker, makes the secrets, picks the ports and starts the bundle. <code>{'--public'}</code> adds a proxy with a certificate and TURN over TLS.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="ws-tile">
      <h3><a href="/how-to/run-with-quadlets/">Quadlets</a></h3>
      <p>systemd units for each container, generated from the same Compose bundle, with the secrets as Podman secrets.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="ws-tile">
      <h3><a href="/how-to/run-edges/">Edges and a CDN</a></h3>
      <p>For a big night: an HLS edge and an audio relay image, steered by the station; or <a href="/how-to/use-bunny/">Bunny</a> in front of it. Measured on one machine so far.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</Feature>

<Feature id="studio-app" kicker="The studio app" title="One window for the desk, the screener, the carts and the week.">
  <p>The studio app is a desktop program for Linux and Windows that signs in to a station over https. Its tabs are the desk, the screener, the cart wall, the chat and the schedule, each reachable by a key, and every panel carries its own name for bug reports and the documentation. Dark by default, with a light theme.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Linux: an AppImage, a <code>.deb</code> and an <code>.rpm</code>; Windows: an installer, unsigned, not yet run on Windows itself.</li>
    <li>A signed updater with a rollback for the AppImage.</li>
    <li>The cart wall: five banks of 24 carts on the letter keys, every cart heard in every return.</li>
  </ul>
  <Shot slot="media" src={carts} alt="The studio app's Carts tab: five banks across the top, Reactions and comedy selected, and a grid of carts on the number and letter keys, such as Applause after a concert on 1, Rimshot on R and Sad trombone on S, with the empty slots saying an upload fills them; the key help runs along the bottom." caption="The cart wall, bank 1: each cart on a key, each key printed on it." />
</Feature>

## Release one

<div class="ws-note not-content">
  <p>WeaveSignal is built in milestones, and this site is written as each lands. Release one has the desk, the studio app, call-in and the join pages, playout and the schedule, video, the chat, show cards and feeds, the public API, and the Cloudron and Compose routes. Not in it: the presenter kit, WHIP for remote DJs, published images and an app-store listing, and the Windows installer run on Windows. Where a page says <strong>planned</strong>, the feature is not built yet.</p>
  <p><a href="/reference/release-one-status/">What is built, and what is planned</a>, milestone by milestone.</p>
</div>
